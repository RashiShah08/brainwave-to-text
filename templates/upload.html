What the subject was asked to do

The recordings come from the PhysioNet EEG Motor Movement/Imagery Database. A target appeared on the screen and the subject responded to it until it disappeared, then relaxed. The target position is what a decode recovers — BCI2000 stored exactly this as its TargetCode. The movement is what the subject did about it.

The two pairs are not the same question. Left and right vary across the midline, so the two classes differ in which hemisphere went quiet. Top and bottom do not: the foot area sits medially, under the vertex, so that pair is lateral against medial and has no left or right in it at all.

Input contract

Enforced, not coerced. A recording that does not match is rejected rather than silently resampled or reordered — either would invalidate the spatial filters the model learned.

Cross-subject is the number that describes this page. It is what an uncalibrated model achieves on a person it has never recorded, which is what happens when you upload a file. Within-subject accuracy — a model refitted on one person's own trials — is always higher and is not what is being served here.

A recording from a subject this model was trained on scores higher still, because the model has seen that person's data. On the PhysioNet subjects it was trained on it reaches about 50%; on someone genuinely new, the cross-subject figure above. Both are far above chance and neither is reliable enough to control anything that matters.
